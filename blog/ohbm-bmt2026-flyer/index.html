FIU Students Bring the Science of Memory to Miami Classrooms Through Global Brain Mapping Initiative

On May 28, 2026, neuroscience came alive for middle school students in Miami as three undergraduate students from Florida International University joined a worldwide movement to make brain science more accessible, engaging, and inspiring for young learners.

As part of Brain Mappers of Tomorrow (BMT) — the annual international outreach initiative led by the Diversity & Inclusivity Committee of the Organization for Human Brain Mapping — the FIU team hosted an interactive one-hour neuroscience outreach session for students at Campbell Drive K–8 Center under the supervision of Dr. Kangjoo Lee.

The FIU event was one of several international satellite sessions coordinated through the BMT initiative by Dr. Leyla Loued-Khenissi, who serves as the lead organizer for Brain Mappers of Tomorrow within the OHBM Diversity & Inclusivity Committee. In addition to supporting satellite outreach programs around the world — including the FIU session in Miami — Dr. Loued-Khenissi is organizing the flagship in-person BMT session that will take place during the OHBM 2026 Annual Meeting, held from June 14–18, 2026, at the Palais 2 l’Atlantique in Bordeaux, France.

🌐 Event Webpage: https://ohbm-dic.github.io/kidsreview/2026/english_session/

Sponsored by the Department of Biomedical Engineering at Florida International University and the Biomedical Engineering Society (BMES) Student Chapter at Florida International University, the event aimed to introduce students to the fascinating world of the human brain, learning, and memory through hands-on science communication designed specifically for young audiences.

Leading the educational presentation were Trinidad Iturriaga and Magda Escobar, who guided students through the foundations of brain anatomy, different forms of learning and memory, and the remarkable ways scientists can observe brain activity using modern neuroimaging technologies.

From understanding how memories are formed to exploring how researchers map brain function in real time, the session transformed complex neuroscience concepts into an exciting and accessible learning experience for the middle school audience.

The presentation was followed by an enthusiastic Q&A session where Campbell Drive students asked thoughtful, curious, and surprisingly insightful questions about the brain, memory, and neuroscience research. Their excitement and eagerness to learn created a lively atmosphere that energized both the presenters and teachers alike. After the discussion, students participated in fun interactive activities that made the experience even more memorable and engaging.

Students, teachers, and the FIU outreach team all shared in the excitement, laughter, and hands-on learning throughout the session. Teachers at Campbell Drive K–8 Center expressed enthusiasm about the students’ engagement, while the FIU team described the outreach experience as both meaningful and incredibly rewarding.

Behind the scenes, Marian Navarro served as the lead moderator and coordinator for the FIU team. Marian worked closely with faculty and school administrators to organize the outreach session, collaborating extensively with Ms. Lema Gilliard to ensure the event’s success. She also played a central role in communication between the FIU team, the OHBM Diversity & Inclusivity Committee, and Campbell Drive, helping develop outreach materials, event flyers, and webpage content for the official BMT platform.

The team also received important behind-the-scenes support from Amy D. Reid, whose guidance and encouragement helped bring the event to fruition. As Assistant Director of the Office of Undergraduate Research and Internships within FIU’s Department of Biological Sciences, Reid supported the students’ outreach efforts and contributed to making the initiative a successful reality for the participating middle school students.

The initiative reflects a growing commitment among young scientists to bridge the gap between advanced neuroscience research and community education. Through programs like Brain Mappers of Tomorrow, students are not only learning about the brain — they are discovering that science can be interactive, approachable, and deeply connected to their everyday lives. By bringing neuroscience directly into local classrooms, these FIU students are helping inspire the next generation of scientists, healthcare innovators, and curious minds.
